Understanding a case

Reports with the same sender and subject line are grouped into one case. Every case shows the email and its headers, everyone who reported it, when it was first and last reported, how many people received it (Accounts Secured), and an audit log of every action taken.

With the ATT add-on, each case also gets a verdict (Phishing, Spam, or Legitimate), a risk score from 0 to 100, Key Evidence for the call, and Recommended Actions.

What action should I take?

Match the situation to the recommended action.

Confirmed phishing
Quarantine

Quarantines every copy in every inbox in your district.

What happens when I quarantine?
  • The action applies to the full case, not only the person who reported it.
  • The action is recorded in the audit log.
  • Quarantine can be reversed from the Quarantine folder.
One harmful email needs to go
Delete

Deletes every copy of that email across your district.

When should I use Delete?
Use Delete when the specific message needs to be removed everywhere, especially when the sender itself may still be legitimate.
Junk or unwanted mail
Review, then Mark Spam

Check that it's junk first. Staff often report real but unwanted mail.

Why review it first?
Newsletters, vendor messages, and fundraiser emails are often reported as spam even when they are legitimate. Manual review helps prevent unnecessary removal.
A real email was reported
Verify, then Mark Safe

Mark Safe trusts the whole sender domain, not only this email.

Before you Mark Safe
Only use Mark Safe when you trust the sender's entire domain, not simply because this one message appears legitimate.
You're not sure
Escalate

Flags the case so another admin can take a look.

It may have spread further
Advanced Threat Search

Finds copies in inboxes of people who never reported it.

What does the search do?
It helps you find additional copies of the message that already exist across your district, including mail that nobody reported. Blocking future mail is a separate action.
Several people reported the same email? Act on the case once. CyberNut groups matching reports together, so one action covers them all.
You can also select several cases and act on them at once.

If you're under attack

Contain first, then determine the blast radius and clean up the rest.

1

Review

Open the case and check the sender, report count, and sent time. With ATT, check the verdict and Key Evidence.

What should I check?
  • Sender and subject
  • Report count
  • Sent date and first/last report
  • ATT verdict and risk score
  • Key Evidence
2

Contain

Quarantine or Delete the case to remove every copy in your district.

Which action should I use?
Use Quarantine for a confirmed threat. Use Delete when you need the specific harmful email removed everywhere.
3

Search

Open Advanced Threat Search from inside the case. The subject line fills in for you.

What should I search?
Set the time frame to cover the original send date so you can find copies received by users who never reported the email.
4

Remediate

Act on every copy the search finds, including ones nobody reported.

What is the goal?
Make sure the response covers the full blast radius, not just the users who originally reported the message.
5

Verify

Confirm Accounts Secured matches who received it, then check the audit log.

What should I verify?
Confirm the expected recipients are covered and review the audit log to see what actions were taken and whether they were manual or automated.
Advanced response considerations
  • Advanced Threat Search doesn't need to be turned on. Open it from inside any case.
  • Advanced Threat Search cleans up mail that already exists. To stop new mail from the sender, block the sender or domain separately. Domain blocking works for Microsoft 365 districts only.
  • If the phishing came from one of your own staff accounts, Compromised Account Detection can flag the account and suspend it within minutes.
  • On-premises Active Directory or Exchange: a directory sync can reactivate a suspended account about every 30 minutes. Suspend the account in your own directory too.
  • Google Workspace: if reinstating an account doesn't carry over to Google, reinstate it in the Google Admin Console.

Leave off at first

Common situations

Open the scenario that matches what you are seeing.

Someone reported phishing
Open the case and check the evidence, the report count, and the sent date. Quarantine the case, then confirm Accounts Secured updated. If the Phishing rule is on, cases at or above your score are quarantined for you, so just spot-check the audit log.
Several employees reported the same email
There's nothing extra to do. Matching reports are already one case, and one action covers them all. Grouping needs the exact same sender and subject, so a different sender address shows up as a separate case.
Someone reported a legitimate email
Make sure the email is real. Before you choose Mark Safe, decide whether you trust everything from that sender's domain, because Mark Safe applies to the whole domain.
Someone reported spam
Preview the email before you act on the label. Newsletters and fundraiser blasts often get reported. If it's junk, Mark Spam. We recommend reviewing spam by hand until you trust the labels.
The attack may have reached more users
Open Advanced Threat Search from inside the case, set the time frame to cover the send date, and remediate every copy it finds.
A trusted vendor sent one harmful email
Delete the case. That removes the one email everywhere without trusting the vendor's domain. Avoid Mark Safe here.
An internal account is sending phishing
Compromised Account Detection flags the account and alerts your admins, and can suspend it within minutes if auto-suspend is on. Quarantine the related case and reset the account's password before you reinstate it.

Before you act

A few details prevent the most common mistakes.

Report, don't forward

A forwarded email never becomes a case, so it can't be grouped, labeled, or removed district-wide.

Mark Safe trusts the domain

Use it only when you trust the sender's whole domain.

Search beyond the reporter

The person who reported an email is rarely the only one who got it.

Search doesn't block future email

Advanced Threat Search removes mail that already exists. Blocking the sender is a separate step.

Actions can be undone

Quarantine, Spam, and Safe actions can be reversed from their folder.
